Your tank is flooding the floor

Once water is escaping through the steel shell, the tank is finished and every minute adds to the mess. Your job is to stop the supply and protect the building until we arrive.

Stop the incoming water

Close the cold inlet valve on the pipe above the tank. If that valve is stuck or leaking, close the main shut-off for the whole property, usually near the meter or where the line enters the building.

Kill power or gas

For an electric heater, flip its dedicated breaker, because a dry element can burn out and wet wiring is dangerous. For gas, turn the control to off. A gas smell means leave the building and call the gas utility or 911.

Pull belongings out of the water

Move boxes, tools and anything else stored on the garage floor. Water spreads under walls faster than most people expect on a flat slab.

Document, then wait for us

Photograph the tank, the label and the water line on the floor. Leave the tank full if you can, since we will drain it safely when we arrive.

In a condo, call your manager as well. A heater failure over a shared wall or an upstairs unit can send water to units below.

From dead tank to hot shower: the replacement sequence

A swap can finish in a single visit when the new unit fits the old connections. These are the stages we work through.

  1. 1

    Confirm the failure

    We check the leak path to make sure the tank shell is the source, not a fitting we can tighten. If a cheaper repair would hold, we say so.

  2. 2

    Drain and disconnect

    We drain the tank to a safe spot, cut the water and fuel connections, and free the vent or electrical feed.

  3. 3

    Set the new unit

    The heater goes into a fresh drain pan with a discharge line, on a level base, with new flexible connectors and shut-off valves where the old ones are tired.

  4. 4

    Add the safety hardware

    We fit a new temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, and an expansion tank when the system needs one.

  5. 5

    Fire it up and test

    We fill the tank, bleed air from the hot lines, check every joint for seepage, verify the gas flame or electric elements and set the thermostat.

Picking the next heater: tank, tankless or hybrid

You are not stuck with the same type you had. A failed tank is a natural time to weigh your options.

Another storage tank

The simplest swap, usually the fastest, and it keeps your existing gas or electric hookup. Capacity matters, so match it to how many people shower back to back.

Tankless

No standing water to leak, and a smaller footprint on the wall. It may need a larger gas line, new venting or an upgraded electrical circuit, so the job grows. See our tankless water heater page for the trade-offs.

Hybrid heat pump

Pulls heat from surrounding air and uses less electricity than a standard electric tank. It needs room around it and a condensate drain, and the unit is taller and costs more up front.

Same-size direct swap versus a change

Switching between gas and electric means new lines, venting or a new circuit. We tell you what that adds before you commit.

How long a tank lasts, and when a repair is a waste

Tanks wear out because the steel liner slowly corrodes from the inside. The anode rod delays this, but once it is spent, rust works through the lining and the shell starts to weep at the seams or the bottom. A tank in that state cannot be repaired, and pushing it a few more weeks risks a full flood.

Age alone does not condemn a heater, and a well-maintained one can outlast a neglected one by years. Still, a unit that is well into its second decade, has already needed an element or valve, and now shows rust at the base is rarely worth another repair. Our water heater repair team will fix a good tank, and we will tell you plainly if yours is not one.

Corroded fittings and a tank that sweats near the bottom are early warnings. Replacing before the failure saves the floor, the drywall and the weekend.

Storms, flooding and Deerfield Beach conditions

Hurricane season runs June through November, and a heater that sat in floodwater or lost power repeatedly may have damaged controls, burners or wiring. Do not restore power or gas to a unit that was underwater. Have us inspect it first, because a wet gas valve or a shorted element is a safety risk.

Many local homes and condos are on slabs, so a garage heater sits right at floor level where water collects. A raised stand and a drain pan with a discharge line cost little compared with a ruined garage, and we install both with every swap. The city uses chloramine and publishes an annual water quality report, so ask the city for it and test your tap if scale worries you. Planned upgrades are covered on our water heater installation page.

Two replacements rarely look alike. We give a clear price before work starts, after we see the space, the fuel and the existing hookups.

Type and capacityA larger tank, a tankless unit or a hybrid costs more than a basic like-for-like swap.
Fuel and connection changesMoving from electric to gas, or the reverse, brings new lines, venting or a circuit.
Code and safety itemsRelief valve, discharge piping, drain pan and expansion tank are part of a proper install.
Location and accessA heater in an attic platform or a tight closet takes more labor than one in an open garage.
Removal and disposalWe haul away the old unit, and heavy or awkward tanks take extra handling.
