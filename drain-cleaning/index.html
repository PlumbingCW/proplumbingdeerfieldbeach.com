Slow kitchen sinks and why they keep coming back

A kitchen drain rarely clogs from one bad moment. Cooking fat cools inside the trap and the horizontal arm behind it, then collects rice, coffee grounds and food film until the pipe is half its original width. That is why a sink that drains slowly for weeks suddenly stops on a busy evening.

We start with the trap and the wall stub, because the blockage is often within a few feet of the cabinet. If the clog sits deeper, a small drum machine with a cable and a cutting head scrapes the pipe wall instead of just poking a hole through the middle. A hole through the middle gives you a few good days, then the slow drain returns.

Double-bowl sinks, garbage disposals and dishwasher hoses share one outlet, so we check all of them. When the disposal feeds the problem, our garbage disposal service covers the unit itself.

Sink full, tub standing, or water coming up from a floor drain

When water refuses to leave, the first hour decides how far it spreads. These steps keep the mess small until we arrive.

Stop adding water

Pause the dishwasher and washing machine, and ask everyone in the building to hold off on flushing and showers. Every gallon that goes down a blocked line has to go somewhere.

Check how many fixtures are affected

One slow sink points to a local clog. If the tub gurgles when a toilet flushes, or a floor drain bubbles, the blockage may be in a shared branch or the main line, and you should tell us so.

Skip the chemical openers

Caustic cleaners sit in a standing clog and can burn skin and splash on the plumber who has to open the trap. Plain hot tap water and a cup plunger are fine to try while you wait.

Protect floors and belongings

Put towels at the base of the fixture, lift rugs, and raise anything stored on the floor of a utility room or garage. Photograph any water that has already escaped.

In a condo or a managed building, a backup can come from a shared stack above you. Call the property manager as you call us, because the cleaning may need access to a neighbor's unit or a common cleanout.

Cable machine or hydro jetter: how we choose

Both tools clear drains, but they work differently and suit different pipes. We pick based on what is blocking the line and what the pipe is made of.

Snaking with a cable

  • Cutting head bores through soft clogs and roots
  • Good for a single fixture or a short run
  • Small machine fits tight cabinets and tub access
  • Leaves a thin film of grease on the pipe wall
  • Usually the first step for a stuck sink or tub

Jetting with high-pressure water

  • Water scours the full pipe wall, not just the center
  • Best for grease, scale and silt that keeps returning
  • Needs a cleanout or an open end to work from
  • Larger equipment, so access matters
  • Details are on our hydro jetting page

If cabling has cleared a line twice and the clog returns, that pattern tells us the pipe needs scouring or a closer look, not another quick pass.

Drains we clear around the house and the property

Each drain type has its own habits. This is what we look for when the call is about one of these.

  • Kitchen sink and laundry standpipe Grease and lint collect in the trap arm, and the washer standpipe backs up when the line behind it narrows.
  • Bathroom sink Hair and toothpaste residue catch on the pop-up stopper and in the trap, so we pull the stopper before we cable anything.
  • Shower and tub Hair wraps around the strainer crossbar and builds a mat just below it, which a hooked tool or a short cable removes.
  • Floor drains Garage, laundry room and utility floor drains fill with silt, lint and debris and often have a dry trap that lets odors rise.
  • Retail and plaza floor drains Shops along W Hillsboro Blvd, including those in strip centers, rely on floor drains and mop sinks that handle mop water, grit and food residue.
  • Outdoor and patio drains Leaves, sand and storm debris settle in the grate and the line below, especially after a long wet summer.
  • Dishwasher air gap A small cap on the sink deck can leak when the line behind it is partly clogged, so we clear the drain before we replace parts.

A dishwasher air gap that spits water onto the counter almost always signals a restricted drain path, not a defective dishwasher.

Slab homes, shared stacks and what they change

Many Deerfield Beach homes and condos sit on concrete slabs, so the drain lines run below the floor instead of through a basement ceiling. That means a stubborn clog may need a cleanout outside the building, and a broken line cannot be reached from below.

In large communities such as Century Village and the Hillsboro Cove and Crystal Lake buildings, several units may feed a common stack. If your tub and your downstairs neighbor's sink back up together, ask the association who handles that stack before anyone cuts into a wall.

When clearing a drain reveals a damaged pipe

Sometimes the cable stops short, snags on a ledge, or brings back chips of clay or cast iron. Those are signs the pipe itself has cracked, sagged or offset, and no amount of cleaning will fix it. We stop, tell you what the tool felt, and recommend a camera inspection so you can see the damage on video.

If the camera confirms a break, our sewer line repair team lays out spot repair, lining or replacement. We would rather tell you a pipe is failing than keep selling cleanings on a line that cannot hold.
