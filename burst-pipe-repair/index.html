Water is spraying right now: what to do in the first minutes

Most of the damage from a burst pipe happens in the minutes after the break, not at the moment it splits. Water keeps flowing until someone stops it, and a failed supply line can move a surprising amount of water into floors, walls and ceilings.

Shut off the main valve

Find the main shut-off where the water line enters the building. In many Deerfield Beach homes it sits near the water heater, in the garage, or on an exterior wall. A lever handle closes with a quarter turn, and a round wheel handle turns clockwise several times.

Drain the lines

Once the main is closed, open one faucet at the lowest point and one at the highest. This drops the pressure left in the pipes and slows the leak while you wait.

Keep power and water apart

If water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, switch off that breaker only if you can reach it without stepping into water. If you cannot, stay clear and tell us when you call so we can plan around it.

Protect and photograph

Lift rugs, move electronics and valuables, and set a bucket under any active drip. Take photos or a short video of the water and the damage before you clean up, because your insurer will ask for them.

Living in a condo or a managed community? Tell the property manager or association right away. A shared riser or stack may need the building's water shut off for several units, and we coordinate the repair with them.

Why pipes split, and where we usually find the break

A burst is rarely random. Old copper can pinhole or split at a worn elbow, fittings let go when water pressure runs high, and exposed lines outdoors take damage from sun, mowers and corrosion. Pipes that run through attics and garages also flex with heat and take hits from stored items.

Deerfield Beach has no freeze risk, so the cold-weather splits that dominate plumbing calls up north are not the usual cause here. Pressure, age, movement and corrosion are the more common culprits. Homes close to the coast, such as those near Main Beach and The Cove, also deal with salt air that attacks exposed metal fittings.

Many local homes and condos sit on concrete slabs, so a supply line often runs inside a wall, above a ceiling or under the floor. Water can travel a long way before it shows, which is why we trace the source before we open anything.

Signs of a cracked or split pipe

Not every burst sprays. Many start as a crack or pinhole and soak the building for days. These are the signs that point to a failed pipe.

Water with no obvious source

A wet floor, a damp wall or a dripping ceiling when no fixture is in use usually means a pressurized line is leaking.

A sudden pressure drop

If showers and taps weaken all at once, a large break upstream may be bleeding water before it reaches the fixtures.

A meter that will not stop

Close every tap and appliance, then watch the water meter. A moving dial means water is leaving the system somewhere.

Stains, bubbling paint or warped trim

Brown rings, blistered paint and swollen baseboards show that moisture has been working behind the surface for a while.

Running water sounds

A hiss or trickle inside a wall at night, when the house is quiet, often marks a small break under pressure.

A musty smell

Damp drywall and insulation smell stale within a day or two in Florida humidity, so an odor with no visible leak deserves a check.

How a burst pipe repair visit goes

Here is the sequence we follow once we arrive. It keeps the repair small and the mess contained.

  1. 1

    Secure the water

    We confirm the main is closed, check that nothing electrical is at risk, and make sure the area is safe to work in.

  2. 2

    Locate the break

    We use listening gear, thermal imaging and a pressure test to find the failure without tearing out more drywall than needed.

  3. 3

    Cut out and replace

  4. 4

    Plan the cleanup

    We explain what dried or damaged areas need next, and which parts of the job you may want to show your insurer.

A valve that will not close, and other breaks that look alike

Sometimes the burst is not in the pipe at all. A corroded shut-off valve can split at its stem or refuse to turn, leaving you with a leak and no way to stop it. We replace stuck or leaking valves so each fixture and zone can be isolated again, and if the failing valve sits under a sink, our faucet and sink repair work covers the supply lines and stops as well.

If you can hear or see signs of a leak but the source stays hidden, leak detection can pinpoint it before we open a wall. And if the break turns out to be in the line between the meter and the house, that is a job for water service line repair.

When we find the pipe is worn throughout, we will say so plainly and talk through repiping as an option, along with what it would involve for your home. We never push a bigger job when a repair will hold.

Condos, HOAs and who pays for what

In gated condo communities such as Century Village, and in many Hillsboro Cove and Crystal Lake buildings, the pipe that burst may belong to the association or to you depending on where it sits. Ask the association for its rules before work starts, because shared stacks and walls often fall under building policy.

What affects the cost of burst pipe repair

Burst pipe jobs range from a small splice to a full run replacement, so we do not quote over the phone without details. We give a clear price before work starts, and these factors shape it.

Pipe material and sizeCopper, PEX and galvanized lines need different parts and different methods.
How much pipe is damagedA single pinhole is a short job, while a corroded run takes longer and uses more material.
Access to the breakA line in an open garage is quick to reach, and one in a finished wall or ceiling takes more careful work.
Valves and fittings involvedReplacing a failed shut-off or a cluster of old fittings adds parts and time.
Permits and inspectionPermit rules vary, so ask the City of Deerfield Beach building department whether your job needs one.

Homeowners policies often cover the sudden water damage a burst pipe causes, but they usually do not pay to replace the worn pipe that failed. Check your policy and call your insurer early.
