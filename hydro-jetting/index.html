A line is backing up and cannot wait for the next shift

Jetting is a fast fix for the right clog, but the steps you take before we arrive protect the building and make the work easier.

Stop feeding the line

Turn off dishwashers, pot fillers and ice machines that drain into the blocked line, and ask staff or residents to avoid flushing. A line that is already full takes in more water and overflows at the lowest drain.

Find the cleanout

Look for a capped pipe near the building wall, in a mechanical room, or in the parking area. Do not open it if water is standing high behind the cap, but tell us where it is so we can start quickly.

Keep people and power clear

Rising floor-drain water near coolers, panels or kitchen equipment is a shock and slip hazard. Block the area, lift stock off the floor, and switch off only what you can reach safely and dry.

Note the pattern

Tell us which drains slowed first, whether grease was recently poured down, and whether the problem has happened before. That history helps us decide how aggressive the cleaning pass should be.

For a leased space in a plaza or a condo building, tell the landlord or association right away. A shared line may need their approval or their key to a common cleanout.

How the jetting visit runs, start to finish

We keep the process predictable so you know what happens to your pipe at each stage.

  1. 1

    Size up the line

    We ask what has been draining, locate the nearest cleanout, and check whether a clear path exists for the hose.

  2. 2

    Camera first when it makes sense

    If a line has failed repeatedly or we suspect damage, we run a camera before the water so we know what the jetter will face.

  3. 3

    Feed the hose with the right nozzle

    We choose a nozzle for the job. A forward-facing tip bores through a stoppage, and rear-facing jets pull the hose along while scouring the walls.

  4. 4

    Work back out slowly

    We clean on the way out as well as the way in, watching for debris returning to the cleanout and adjusting pressure to the pipe material.

  5. 5

    Confirm flow and report

    We run water through the line, check that the fixtures drain normally, and describe what we removed and what we saw.

Jobs where high-pressure water earns its keep

A hydro jetter is a specialist tool. These are the situations where it usually beats a cable on results.

Grease and scale in kitchen lines

Fat hardens into a waxy lining that a cable only pokes through. Hot, high-volume water strips the lining and restores the pipe's full diameter.

Tree roots and silt in main lines

Fine roots enter at joints and trap paper and silt behind them. A cutting nozzle clears the roots and flushes the sediment out together.

Yard drains and culvert pipes

Sand, leaves and mud wash into catch basins during heavy summer rain. Jetting pushes the buildup out of the run so the next storm drains properly.

Restaurants and food service

Grease interceptor inlets, prep sinks and floor drains see daily loading. Scheduled jetting keeps these lines open between slow periods.

Shops, offices and plazas

Shared building lines collect everything from every tenant. A periodic scouring prevents one tenant's problem from flooding another's floor.

Lines that clear, then fail again

Repeated clogs in the same spot suggest a coating or a ledge. Jetting cleans the whole run so we can see whether the pipe is sound underneath.

Honest limits and when we camera first

Hydro jetting will not fix a collapsed pipe, a belly that holds water, or a joint that has pulled apart. In a weak clay or aged cast iron line, strong pressure can make an existing crack worse. That is why we run a sewer camera inspection before jetting whenever the history or the first look raises doubts.

If the camera shows damage, we explain what the water would have done and recommend sewer line repair instead. We would rather skip a jetting job than sell one that cannot succeed, and in lines with shared stacks, we also make sure the association knows what is planned.

What affects the price of hydro jetting

Jetting costs more than a basic cable pass because the equipment is larger and the setup takes longer. We give a clear price before work starts so the scope is settled first.

Insurance rarely pays for jetting as routine maintenance. If a backup damaged floors, drywall or stock, your policy may cover that damage, so check it and take photos before cleanup.

Businesses should ask the landlord who is responsible for the shared building line, because the lease often decides who pays.
