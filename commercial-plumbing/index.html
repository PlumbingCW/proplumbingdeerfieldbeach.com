Your business has a flood, a backup, or no water at all

Commercial plumbing emergencies come with extra pressure, because customers, tenants and food safety may all be affected. A calm first ten minutes can protect your floors, your stock and your opening hours.

Isolate the area

Close the nearest shut-off for the fixture or branch, or the main valve if the source is unclear. Know where the main is before an emergency, and make sure staff do too.

Stop using the drains

If a floor drain or sink is backing up, stop dishwashing, mopping into drains and flushing. Every extra gallon adds to the overflow.

Protect people and power

Block off wet floors, shut down equipment that stands in water, and keep a hand off electrical panels if the floor is wet.

Document and notify

Photograph the water and the damage, and notify the landlord, property manager or association if you lease the space. They may need to shut off a shared riser or arrange access.

If sewage is involved, treat the area as contaminated. Keep food and people out until it has been cleared and cleaned.

Business types we plumb, and what each one needs

Plumbing needs differ from a restaurant kitchen to an office suite. These are the main categories.

Restaurants and food service

Grease lines, floor drains, hand sinks and prep sinks work hard all day. A grease-heavy line slowly narrows until a Friday night backup, and our hydro jetting scours it clean.

Retail, offices and plazas

Restrooms, mop sinks and small water heaters are the usual trouble spots. A leaking supply line above a ceiling tile is one of the common after-hours calls.

Multifamily and managed properties

Shared stacks, risers and many units mean one leak can affect several. We work with managers, and invoice per unit or per property when that helps your records.

Floor drains and lift pumps

A floor drain that overflows or a sump or lift pump that fails can flood a back room fast. We clear the drain and service the pump or float switch.

Backflow assemblies

These devices protect the public water supply from reverse flow. Ask your water utility about testing rules, and we handle repair or replacement of the device.

Business water heaters

A failed tank in a salon, shop or kitchen can close the door for the day. Our water heater replacement team sizes a unit for your demand.

Plazas, strip centers and storefronts along W Hillsboro Blvd

Our office sits on W Hillsboro Blvd, so we know the kind of retail and service buildings that line it, including strip centers like Deer Run and the Shoppes at Deerfield. In a plaza, several tenants often share a roof, a slab and sometimes a drain line. A blockage in the shared line can show up as a backup in the unit farthest downstream, even though the cause sits elsewhere.

That is why a good commercial call starts with questions about the building. Who owns the line, which tenants share it, where the cleanout sits, and what the last service was. A camera look through a cleanout often answers it, and our leak detection work finds slow leaks behind finished walls without opening them blindly.

Restaurants and food shops are the most grease-exposed tenants. A grease interceptor, where one exists, needs regular pumping, and kitchen lines downstream of it still pick up residue. We explain what is ours to fix and what the landlord or a hauler handles.

Emergency service or a planned maintenance visit

Many commercial calls could have been scheduled days earlier. Here is how the two paths differ.

Emergency, after-hours call

  • Overflowing drains, active leaks or no water at all
  • Same-day visit when available, often outside business hours
  • Fix focused on stopping damage and restoring service
  • Larger repairs may be staged for a planned visit
  • Best when waiting would harm stock or safety

Planned service visit

  • Slow drains, minor leaks, worn valves
  • Scheduled for slow hours to protect your trade
  • Includes camera or jetting before a blockage forms
  • Gives time to compare options and plan the budget
  • Best for leak surveys and aging equipment

A scheduled line cleaning before your busy season usually costs less than a Saturday night backup.

Living with salt air, storms and humidity as a business owner

Businesses near the coast, close to Main Beach, the International Fishing Pier and The Cove, deal with salt air that attacks exposed fittings, valves and rooftop equipment. Look over exposed supply lines and shut-offs at least once a year, and replace any that show crust or green corrosion before they seize.

Hurricane season runs from June through November, so it pays to know where your main shut-off is and to confirm it still turns. Heavy summer rain can also load drains and lift stations. If you manage a property, ask your tenants to report slow drains early.

Storm damage to pipes, roof drains or equipment sometimes shows up weeks later as a stain or a damp wall. A leak survey after a major storm can catch it before the mold does. Our Coconut Creek page explains how we cover businesses farther west.
