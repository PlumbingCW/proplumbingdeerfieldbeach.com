Water Heater Leaking From the Bottom in Deerfield Beach, FL

A water heater leaking from the bottom is either a fixable part, such as a drain valve or a fitting, or a failed tank that needs replacing. Shut off the power or gas and close the cold water supply first, then look at where the water actually starts.

Water at the base of the tank body, from a seam or a rusted bottom, almost always means the tank has failed. Water from a valve, a pipe connection or a drain pan can often be repaired, so the source matters more than the puddle.

By the Deerfield Beach Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 23, 2026Updated October 23, 2026

Shut it down first, then read the puddle

Before you look for the cause, stop the leak from growing. For an electric unit, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control to the off or pilot position, and close the cold water shut-off valve on the pipe above the tank.

Then check where the water is really coming from. Wipe the base dry, put a dry paper towel under each connection, and watch for a minute. A damp towel under a pipe fitting tells a different story than a steady drip from the bottom edge of the tank.

Do not drain the tank unless you have a plan for where the water will go, and never touch the unit if water is around electrical parts. If the area is flooded or you are unsure, leave it alone and call us. A leaking tank in a closet can soak walls and flooring quickly.

Five common reasons water collects under a tank

Each cause leads to a different repair. Here is how to tell them apart.

A leaking drain valve

The drain valve sits low on the tank and can drip when its seal wears or sediment holds it open slightly. It is a small part, and replacing it is usually a quick repair.

A failed tank

Over time the steel liner corrodes and the tank can seep from the bottom or a seam. This is not repairable, and the unit must be replaced.

A temperature and pressure relief valve

This safety valve opens when pressure or heat climbs too high. If it drips constantly or its discharge pipe runs, there is a pressure or valve problem to check.

Loose or worn fittings

The hot and cold connections at the top can drip and send water running down the tank, so it looks like a bottom leak. Tightening or replacing the fitting often fixes it.

Condensation

In humid weather, a cold tank can sweat. Gas units with high efficiency can also produce condensation that drains out, so a small amount of moisture is sometimes normal.

Real condensation dries up and does not return once the surface warms. If the puddle grows over hours, treat it as a leak.

What Deerfield Beach homeowners should know

Many homes and condos in Deerfield Beach are built on concrete slabs with few or no basements, so water heaters often sit in garages, closets or utility rooms. A leak there runs straight onto the slab or into a carpeted room, which is why a drain pan and a quick shut-off matter.

Garages get hot in summer, and humidity and salt air near the coast can speed up the corrosion on fittings and tank bottoms. If your unit is close to Main Beach or The Cove, check the fittings and the base more often for rust.

The city's water report lists chloramine as its disinfectant. Ask the city for its annual water quality report if you want to talk through what that means for your heater, and do not assume anything about mineral buildup without testing your tap.

Why a leak at the tank body usually means replacement

Inside a tank heater, an anode rod is meant to corrode first so the steel tank does not. When the rod is used up, minerals and water begin to attack the liner, and eventually the bottom or a seam lets go. By the time water shows at the base of the tank, the damage is already through the steel.

No patch will hold on a pressurized tank, and a spot treatment only delays the real failure. Repairing the tank itself is not something we recommend, because the next leak tends to arrive at a worse time.

That does not mean every leak means a new heater. A dripping valve or fitting is a repair, and we will tell you plainly which side your unit falls on. Our water heater repair visit starts by finding the source before anything is replaced.

Repair or replace: what points each way

Use this to size up your situation before you call.

Usually a repair

  • Water comes from a valve, pipe fitting or discharge pipe
  • The tank body is dry and the metal looks clean
  • The unit is not old and has had few repairs
  • The leak is a slow drip at a connection
  • A failed part is a small, cheap component

Usually a replacement

  • Water seeps from the base, a seam or the tank bottom
  • The tank is rusted or flaking at the base
  • The heater is old and has needed repeated repairs
  • The floor around it is soaked or rotting
  • A repair would cost a large share of a new unit

When replacement is the answer, we can often arrange it as a same-day job when available. Our water heater replacement page explains the options, including tank, tankless and hybrid heat pump models.

What to do in the first hour of a bottom leak

These steps limit damage while you wait for a plumber.

  1. 1

    Cut the power or gas

    Turn off the breaker for an electric unit or set a gas unit to off. Do this before you touch anything near the water.

  2. 2

    Close the cold water supply

    Turn the shut-off valve on the cold pipe at the top of the tank to stop new water from entering.

  3. 3

    Contain the water

    Mop up what you can, move stored items and place towels or a bucket. A wet vacuum helps if you have one and the area is safe.

  4. 4

    Photograph the damage

    Take photos of the unit, the label and the wet area before cleanup. Insurers usually ask for them.

  5. 5

    Call a licensed plumber

    Describe where the water starts. That helps us bring the right parts and decide whether to repair or replace.

Condo closets, drain pans and who pays for damage below

A drain pan under the tank catches small leaks and sends them to a drain line. It buys you time but does not stop a failed tank from flooding the room, and a clogged pan line can overflow too. If your heater sits on a platform in a closet, check that the pan is not holding old water.

Water in a condo can damage the unit below, which raises the question of who pays. The answer depends on the association rules and your policy, so read both and notify the manager quickly. Photos and a written description of the repair help every claim.

Homeowner policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage but usually not the replacement of the worn heater itself. Check your policy before you decide anything.
