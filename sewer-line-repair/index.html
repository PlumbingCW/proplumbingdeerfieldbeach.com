Sewage is coming up or the yard is sinking

A damaged sewer line is different from an ordinary clog because the pipe itself is failing. These steps limit health risk and damage until we arrive.

Stop all water use

Every flush or shower sends more wastewater toward a broken pipe. Ask everyone in the building to stop, including neighbors who share the line.

Keep people and pets away

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and animals out of wet areas, wear gloves if you must touch anything, and wash hands thoroughly afterward.

Look outside

A wet, sunken or foul-smelling patch of lawn, or a cleanout that is overflowing, tells us roughly where the line has failed. Take a photo and mark the spot, but do not dig.

Do not use drain chemicals

A broken line cannot be cleared with liquid cleaners, and the chemicals only make later work more hazardous.

In a condo or townhome, call the association too. A failed lateral serving several units is a building matter, and they will need to approve any dig or repair.

How a sewer pipe fails

Sewer lines are out of sight until something goes wrong. These are the failures we see most often.

Cracked or broken pipe

Soil movement, heavy loads and age crack rigid pipe. Wastewater leaks out, and soil can wash in and block the flow.

Offset or separated joints

When sections shift out of line, a lip forms that snags paper and solids. Repeated clogs at the same spot are the classic sign.

Root intrusion

Roots follow moisture to a joint, grow inside the pipe and slowly choke it. Cutting them clears the pipe, but the opening remains.

Bellies and sags

A low spot holds water and waste, so the line clogs after every few uses even though it is not broken.

Collapsed sections

A pipe that has caved in cannot be cleared by any tool. Water backs up and does not recover after a cleaning.

Corrosion in older metal lines

Cast iron flakes and thins from the inside, narrowing the bore until it is more rust than pipe.

Why repairs here come with local complications

Many Deerfield Beach homes and condos sit on concrete slabs, so part of the drain system runs beneath the floor. A break under the slab can mean opening the concrete, or finding another path for the line.

Yards near the Hillsboro Canal and other waterways can sit above a high water table. Wet soil makes trenches unstable, and groundwater can seep into a dig and slow the work. We plan for it by pumping the trench and shoring where needed.

Large trees are the other local factor. Roots from mature trees in a front yard can find a joint and invade it, so we look at where the tree sits in relation to the line before we recommend a fix.

Trenchless lining or open trench

Both methods can restore a line. Neither is always the better choice, and we explain which suits your property.

Trenchless pipe lining

  • Cured liner forms a new pipe inside the old one
  • Smaller access holes, less yard and driveway disruption
  • Works best when the pipe is still in line
  • Does not suit a badly collapsed or offset pipe
  • Can slightly reduce the pipe's inner diameter

Open trench replacement

  • New pipe replaces the old from end to end
  • Fixes bellies, offsets and collapses directly
  • Needs digging through lawns, landscaping or hardscape
  • Wet ground may slow the work and add shoring
  • Surface restoration is a separate step

No method is a promise of how long a pipe will last. We explain why we favor one approach for your line and what could change that recommendation once we see more.

Repair, lateral replacement, or both

A short crack or one bad joint is often a spot repair. If the camera shows several defects, a belly, or pipe that is failing along its length, replacing the whole lateral from the building to the connection is usually the better long-term answer. Patching one spot on a failing line only moves the problem down the pipe.

Roots add a wrinkle. If they entered through a single failed joint, we can repair that joint. If many joints have separated, ongoing cleaning will keep recurring, and our sewer line cleaning service can hold the line open only as a stopgap while you plan the larger fix.

Condo owners should know that a lateral serving several units often belongs to the association. We work with managers on scope and access, and our Coconut Creek page explains how we serve multi-unit properties to the west.
