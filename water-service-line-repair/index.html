The pipe between the meter and your front door

The service line is the underground pipe that brings water from the meter to your building. You cannot see most of it, which is why trouble often shows up as a side effect. Pressure drops, the lawn stays soaked after a dry week, or a meter runs while every tap is closed.

A line can fail because of age, corrosion, ground movement, tree roots pressing on it or damage from digging. A fitting at the meter or at the house wall is a frequent weak point. The leak may be small, but it runs day and night under pressure, so the water loss adds up.

We repair a damaged section when the rest of the line is sound, and we replace the whole run when the pipe is worn throughout. Our team will tell you which one fits, and why, before work begins.

No water at the house, or water pouring from the yard

A failed service line can leave you without water or flood part of the property. A few quick actions protect the building and help us diagnose the problem faster.

Find out where the water is going

Look for a wet or sunken patch between the meter and the house. Pooling water near the meter box or along the building wall tells us where to start.

Close your own shut-off

Turn off the main valve where the line enters your building. If the valve at the meter is the utility's, leave it alone and tell us what you see instead.

Check the meter dial

With the house valve closed, watch the dial or leak indicator. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and your valve, and the fix is on your side.

Check neighbors and the utility

If nearby homes have no water either, the problem may be a utility main issue. The City of Deerfield Beach utility can tell you whether work is underway in your area.

Keep your water bill and any photos of the wet area. They help us and your insurer see when the problem began.

Repair a section or replace the whole line

Both approaches have a place. The right call depends on the pipe, the soil and the history of the line.

Spot repair

  • Fixes one cracked section or failed fitting
  • Needs a smaller excavation
  • Suits a line that is otherwise sound
  • Costs less up front
  • May not stop a second failure nearby

Full replacement

  • Installs new pipe from meter to house
  • Needs a longer trench or a bore
  • Suits old, corroded or often repaired lines
  • Costs more up front
  • Resolves the line as a long-term concern

We can often bore or pull a new line to limit yard damage, but ground conditions and obstacles decide what is possible. We explain the realistic options after seeing the site.

Signs the service line is the problem

These clues point to the buried line more than to indoor plumbing.

  • A meter that moves with everything off Water is escaping somewhere past the meter, and the buried line is a prime suspect.
  • A wet, spongy or sunken yard patch Ground that stays soaked in dry weather often sits above a leak.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure Pressure that falls across the whole house suggests a loss upstream of your fixtures.
  • Dirty or sandy water A damaged line can pull in soil or stir up sediment when pressure shifts.
  • A hissing sound near the meter or wall Escaping water makes noise, especially on a quiet night.
  • A bill that rises with no other cause Run the meter test, and let our leak detection service find the exact spot.

No single sign proves the service line is at fault. We confirm with a pressure test and listening gear before any digging.

Who owns which part, and how to check your line

Typically the meter divides city and owner responsibility, so the line on the street side belongs to the utility and the line from the meter to your building belongs to you. Ask the City of Deerfield Beach utility to confirm for your property before you pay for any work.

The city publishes a service-line material inventory at dfb.city/waterline, tied to its water meter upgrade program, so you can check what material your line is. If the inventory lists your line as lead or unknown, call us to talk through replacement. We do not claim any particular line is lead, only that you deserve to know what is in the ground.

Wherever the line meets the meter, the details matter. A leak at the meter itself is usually the utility's to handle, and we help you work out which side it is on.

What drives the price of a service line repair

No two lines are alike, so a fair quote starts with a look at the site. We give a clear price before work starts, and these factors shape it.

Length of the lineA short line from a close meter costs less to replace than a long run across a large lot.
Pipe material and sizeCopper, plastic and older steel lines need different parts and methods.
What sits above the pipeDriveways, walkways, landscaping and trees add work and restoration time.
Method usedTrenching, boring and pulling a new line each bring different equipment and time.
Permits and inspectionPermit rules vary, so ask the City of Deerfield Beach building department whether your job needs one.

Homeowners policies sometimes offer optional service line coverage, and many do not. Check your policy for a buried line endorsement, since coverage is often an add-on and not part of the standard terms.

Gradual leaks and worn pipes are often excluded. Keep your bills and our written findings in case the insurer asks.
